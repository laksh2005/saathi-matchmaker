# Saathi matching logic

## AI integration

AI sits on top of a deterministic engine rather than replacing it. The engine decides who is eligible and gives every candidate a transparent, explainable score. The matchmaker can then ask the "AI matchmaker" for a second opinion on the top 10: the model (OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini` by default) reads both full profiles, the engine's per factor scores and an optional free text brief from the matchmaker's last call ("she wants someone who loves travel, prefers Pune"). It returns its own fit score, a one line headline, a two sentence reason and an icebreaker for the first conversation. The matchmaker can rank by a 60/40 blend of engine and AI scores, which lets soft, unstructured context influence the order without letting the model override hard preferences. The model also drafts a personal intro email for every Send Match, which the matchmaker edits before sending. Only candidates who already pass the engine's dealbreakers are ever sent to the model, contact details are never included in prompts, and every AI call falls back to rule based explanations and a template email, so the tool works fully without an API key.

## Algorithm design

Matching runs in four steps. (1) Hard filters remove dealbreakers in both directions: same gender, either person's partner age range (with one year of grace), same religion only, caste preference unless both are caste no bar, firm opposite views on children, manglik mismatch when a horoscope match is required, and different countries when neither will relocate. (2) The client's view is a weighted average of 0 to 1 factor scores. For men the brief's rules carry the most weight, as smooth curves instead of binary cut offs: she is ideally 1 to 5 years younger, earns less (score decays as her income passes his), is 5 to 20 cm shorter, and shares his view on children (the heaviest factor). For women, guided by how matrimonial platforms and matchmakers describe what women prioritise, the heaviest factors are career and education parity, profession affinity (fields that understand each other's schedules), relocation fit (who has to move), and family expectations (for example a traditional joint family scores low for a liberal client), with age and height as a light preference. Both share lifestyle (diet, smoking, drinking), family values, language, interests, pets and marital history. (3) The same model is run from the candidate's side, and the final score is 70% client view plus 30% candidate view. This reciprocal step, borrowed from two sided recommender research and from how Hinge and Bumble rank, avoids suggesting people who would never say yes back. (4) A small boost for verified profiles, then ranking and tiers: High Potential (87+), Good Match (77+) and Worth Exploring. Each result carries highlights, concerns and the full factor breakdown so the matchmaker can see why.

## Assumptions

Gender is binary and matches are opposite gender, as the brief specifies. Income is annual, in lakh rupees, and comparable across countries for this MVP. Stated partner age ranges are honest and religion and caste preferences are the client's own, so they are treated as filters rather than scores. Manglik status only matters when someone requires a horoscope match. Weights are hand tuned starting points that a real deployment would learn from outcomes (accepted introductions, first meetings, engagements). The data is a seeded, synthetic pool of 240 profiles (120 per gender) with distributions loosely modelled on urban Indian matrimonial users, and matchmaker edits (journey stage, notes, sent matches) persist in the browser because the demo host has no database.
